TrailForge v0.20.0, an off-road vehicle prototype on a small custom C++20 engine (SDL3 + SDL GPU, Jolt Physics
`WheeledVehicleController`, Dear ImGui, own audio mixer on miniaudio). This stage replaces the blockout boxes of
Exploration Peaks with **real buildings**: an Alpine village and buildings at every point of interest, built from a
procedural kit whose collision comes from the same parts as the visible walls. The vehicle physics did not change.

**Platforms:**

| Package | Status |
|---|---|
| `TrailForge-0.20.0-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS 27 on Apple Silicon. **Run and verified** (built for macOS 27.0). |

## What is new

- **Valley Village.** 14 houses in six variants face the village square. They differ in size, number of floors
  (1–3) and the direction they face. The houses have:
  - stone or plaster ground floors and timber or plaster upper floors;
  - balconies, shutters, and framed windows with dark glass;
  - chimneys, roof overhangs, and clay tile or slate roofs, with gutters and downpipes.

  Around them are gardens with raised beds, fences, a well, benches and a power line along the road.
- **Points of interest:**
  - a fuel station: canopy, pump islands, shop and price sign;
  - a workshop;
  - castle ruins: walls with a breach, two broken towers and a gate;
  - a lighthouse with the keeper's house;
  - a lattice radio mast with its equipment hut;
  - quarry machinery: crusher, conveyor, silo and a rusty shed;
  - docks that reach into the Alpine Lake and the sea, and a boathouse;
  - beach and island camps with tents, a shelter, a woodpile and a fire pit;
  - a forest cabin, a hut at the mountain pass, and cairns and signposts.
- **Honest collision.** Every solid part of a building (wall, roof slab, post, beam, deck, plinth) is also a convex
  collision piece, and the far LOD draws exactly those parts. Up close, the window and door recesses are at most
  16 cm deep, and trims (frames, sills, shutters, flower boxes) stand at most 30 cm proud. Power line wires, antennas
  above 6 m and thin ladder rungs have no collision. New tests check this for every building kind.
- **Placement.** Buildings keep clear of the roads, stay out of the water and off the spawn points. Every foundation
  reaches the ground, and none is buried in a slope.
- **Materials.** 11 CC0 materials from Poly Haven: stucco, rubble stone, castle stone, plank siding, planks, clay
  tiles, slates, corrugated iron, metal, rusty metal and concrete. They add 19.2 MB.
- **Map.** Building footprints now show on the full-screen map and the minimap.
- **Performance** at 2880 × 1800 on a village overview with VSync off, against v0.19.0 (two runs of each build; the
  numbers vary from run to run):

  | Preset | v0.19.0 | v0.20.0 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Low | 120 fps (display cap) | 120 fps |
  | Medium | 120 fps | 120 fps |
  | High | 116–117 fps | 100–113 fps |
  | Ultra | 79–87 fps | 62–83 fps |

## How to open it

**macOS.** The app is **ad-hoc signed, not notarized**, so Gatekeeper blocks a plain double-click after downloading.

1. Unzip `TrailForge-0.20.0-macos-arm64.zip`.
2. Either right-click `TrailForge.app` → **Open** → **Open**, or remove the quarantine flag in Terminal:
   `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine TrailForge.app`
3. Optional command-line options (run the binary inside the bundle):
   `TrailForge.app/Contents/MacOS/trailforge --help`

**Windows (untested on real hardware).** Unzip, keep the `assets` folder next to `trailforge.exe`, run it. The exe
is not code-signed, so SmartScreen may warn ("More info" → "Run anyway"). Needs a Direct3D 12 or Vulkan GPU driver.

**Linux (untested on real hardware).** `tar xzf TrailForge-0.20.0-linux-x64.tar.gz`, then
`./TrailForge-0.20.0-linux-x64/trailforge`. Needs a Vulkan driver (Mesa RADV / ANV / NVK or the NVIDIA driver).

## Known limitations

- Buildings were judged on still captures only.
- The buildings have no interiors and cannot be destroyed. The windows are dark glass that reflects the sky, not
  the scene.
- No glTF props were added; fences, benches and camp items are built by the kit.
- The extra frame cost on High and Ultra is not pinned down: one pair of runs showed about +1 ms of GPU time, the
  other +4 ms on High and +8 ms on Ultra.
- Windows and Linux have never been run on real hardware; the Direct3D 12 path has not been run at all.
- macOS build not notarized; Windows build not code-signed.
- Earlier limitations still apply:
  - water has no physics; deep water and the map edge put the vehicle back on a road;
  - snow, sand and mud are rigid surfaces with lower grip;
  - grass, ground cover, spray and body mud are render-only;
  - ABS and TC are simplified logic;
  - sounds were judged from capture analysis, not by ear;
  - nobody has driven with a real gamepad;
  - TAA, motion blur, clouds and waves were judged on stills only.
