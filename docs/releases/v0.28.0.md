TrailForge v0.28.0, an off-road vehicle prototype on a small custom C++20 engine (SDL3 + SDL GPU, Jolt Physics
`WheeledVehicleController`, Dear ImGui, own audio mixer on miniaudio). This stage reworks the suspension so that a
30–40 cm bump at 30 km/h no longer launches and flips the cars, replaces the dull tire "thud" on landings with a
whole-vehicle sound, and migrates saved garage setups to the new anti-roll units.

**Platforms:**

| Package | Status |
|---|---|
| `TrailForge-0.28.0-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS 27 on Apple Silicon. **Run and verified** (built for macOS 27.0). |

There are no Windows or Linux packages for this release: CI is paused (GitHub Actions billing), so nothing was
built or tested on those platforms. Windows and Linux have never been run on real hardware in any release.

**Private build:** Ironwall Stadium and the IRONCLAD G and KESTREL M bodies are third-party content. Their licence is
unknown or missing. They are included only while the repository is private, and they will be removed or replaced
before the repository or any build is made public. Do not share this package outside the project.

## What is new

- **Suspension rework (all vehicles).** The cars were too bouncy and could flip over a single bump. Causes found:
  Jolt's anti-roll bar acted as a velocity correction whose real stiffness changed with the damper setting and the
  physics step, the bump stops reacted one step late and added energy, the SUV had equal compression and rebound
  damping, and the tire had no give once the suspension was fully compressed. Now:
  - suspension stiffness and damping are set every physics step from that step's wheel contact;
  - anti-roll bars are real forces in N/m at the wheel (values converted to keep the old cornering roll);
  - progressive bump stops over the last 3–6 cm of travel that lose half their force on the way back (that 50 % is
    an assumption, not a measured rubber);
  - dampers with more rebound than compression that soften above 0.25 m/s (SUV 0.35 / 0.55 of critical,
    was 0.45 / 0.45);
  - the tire gives 5–13 cm more (120–300 kN/m) once the suspension is solid.
  - Rollovers in the headless bump probe (0.30 / 0.35 / 0.40 m bumps at 30 km/h, 18 runs per driver; coast /
    frozen throttle / speed held / full throttle), before → after: SUV 0/0/0/0 → 0/0/0/0, EV 0/0/0/1 → 0/0/0/0,
    Desert Patrol Buggy 4/1/6/10 → 1/0/1/5, IRONCLAD G 2/2/7/7 → 0/0/4/5, KESTREL M 0/0/0/2 → 0/0/0/1. Coasting or
    at a constant throttle, no vehicle rolls over a 0.35 m bump at 30 km/h any more.
  - Braking, acceleration and climbs stay within about 1 %, lap times within 1.5 % (mini track: SUV 59.35 s, EV 57.73 s, was
    58.51 s; IRONCLAD G 56.15 s; KESTREL M 50.67 s).
- **Bump strip on the proving ground** (teleport **Bump strip 0.35 m**): two 0.35 m high, 2.5 m long mounds, one
  across both wheel tracks and one under the left wheels only.
- **Landing sounds.** The loud, dull tire "thud" on every load step is gone. Hard landings and big bumps now play a
  short synthesized body rattle (from about 2.3 g of vertical acceleration felt by the body); the bump stops knock
  only when hit fast (from 1.2 m/s), and the recorded slam samples play only on hard hits, high-passed. Each vehicle
  has a body sound type (SUV, tube frame for the buggy, coupe). In the landing test scenario, 27 thuds became 11
  rattles, and the rock crawl at 10 km/h no longer triggers any body sound.
- **Saved garage setups are migrated.** Setup files are now version 2 (anti-roll bars in N/m). A setup saved by an
  earlier version stored the anti-roll bar in the old bar units, which have no fixed conversion to N/m. On the first
  start, the garage drops those two anti-roll values so the vehicle's own bars apply, keeps every other value, shows a
  message, and keeps the original file next to it as `<vehicle>.json.schema1.bak`.

## Verification

- 108 automated tests pass in Debug and Release (97 in the headless build). New tests cover the suspension force
  model (damper ratios, bump-stop force, roll with and without bars), no rollover of any shipped vehicle over a
  0.35 m mound and a 1 m kicker at 30 km/h, the landing sounds, and the setup migration.
- Full regression scenario: 248 passed, 0 failed (Release, no audio).
- Feature scenarios (Release, 0 failures each): bump strip 47 (no audio; every vehicle upright, largest tilt
  12–24°), garage 86 (no audio); landing audio 20, audio check 41, tire squeal 44 (with offline audio capture).
- Sound claims come from offline capture analysis only.

## How to open it

**macOS.** The app is **ad-hoc signed, not notarized**, so Gatekeeper blocks a plain double-click after downloading.

1. Unzip `TrailForge-0.28.0-macos-arm64.zip`.
2. Either right-click `TrailForge.app` → **Open** → **Open**, or remove the quarantine flag in Terminal:
   `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine TrailForge.app`
3. Optional command-line options (run the binary inside the bundle):
   `TrailForge.app/Contents/MacOS/trailforge --help`

## Known limitations

- **Nobody has driven the new suspension by hand.** It was checked only with probes, tests, scenarios and still
  captures.
- IRONCLAD G (only 8–10 cm of bump travel, the body hits) and the Desert Patrol Buggy (750 kg) can still flip with the
  throttle held or floored over a short, steep kicker or a step. A 1 m long, 0.35 m high kicker has a 47° face and
  acts as a ramp. KESTREL M's low nose stops on a 0.35 m mound under one side.
- Not simulated: drivetrain and brake torque pushing back on the body, tire relaxation and load sensitivity, tire
  give before the suspension is fully compressed.
- A dropped anti-roll value in a migrated setup loses that setting; set it again on the slider (now in N/m).
- Nobody has listened to the new landing sounds; the rattle and knock are synthesized, their levels set by
  measurement.
- IRONCLAD G, KESTREL M and Ironwall Stadium are third-party content with an unknown or missing licence (private
  build only, see above).
- No Windows or Linux packages for this release; those platforms have never been run on real hardware.
- macOS build not notarized.
- Earlier limitations still apply:
  - water has no physics; deep water and the map edge put the vehicle back on a road;
  - snow, sand, mud and gravel are rigid surfaces with lower grip;
  - grass, ground cover, spray and body mud are render-only;
  - ABS and TC are simplified logic;
  - tire temperatures are probably below track-day readings;
  - nobody has driven with a real gamepad;
  - buildings have no interiors.
