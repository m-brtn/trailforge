TrailForge v0.21.0, an off-road vehicle prototype on a small custom C++20 engine (SDL3 + SDL GPU, Jolt Physics
`WheeledVehicleController`, Dear ImGui, own audio mixer on miniaudio). This stage adds the **game-side import path for
vehicle bodies modelled in Blender** (`body.glb`): a documented contract, a converter that validates the body against
the physics wheels, the loader and renderer, collision from authored convex pieces, and headless Blender scripts.
The shipped SUV and EV still use their procedural bodies, and their physics did not change.

## What is new

- **Blender bodies (`body.glb`).** A vehicle can point to a body modelled in Blender with
  `"body": {"model": "body.glb"}` in its `vehicle.json`. The body is only the body: wheels, tires and suspension are
  still drawn by the game from the physics wheels. Each named node is a part. Materials map to game roles (paints,
  cladding, glass, chrome, lamps, ...). Head, tail / brake, reverse and indicator lamps follow the vehicle state,
  and the paint can be changed with `body.paint`. If the file fails to load, the game falls back to the procedural
  body. The full contract is in the README, "Blender bodies (body.glb)".
- **Collision from the model.** `COL_*` nodes are convex collision pieces. The physics body is made of their hulls,
  while mass, inertia and centre of mass stay those of the definition.
- **Converter with validation** (`trailforge_vehiclec`). It turns a Blender glTF export into `body.glb` and builds
  two simplified LODs. It refuses a body that:
  - has wheel, tire or suspension parts;
  - has a material without a known role;
  - has a collision piece that is not convex;
  - comes within 10 mm of the space a tire sweeps (full suspension travel, steering lock, every kit, both ends of
    the garage ride height);
  - has collision that does not match the visual body.
- **Blender scripts** (`tools/cars/blender/`). One script writes a starting `.blend` for a vehicle: the concept
  sheet as reference planes, proxy wheels at physics size, wheel-arch cutters sized from the tire and suspension
  travel, a base body with contract names, and collision pieces. A second script exports and converts the body.
  Both run headless or from a live Blender session.
- **Test vehicle.** `tests/data/vehicles/suv_glb_test` (not in the game's vehicle list) is the SUV with a body made
  by these scripts and red paint. It proves the path end to end and is not a shipped design.

## Verification

- 81 automated tests pass in Debug and Release. Six are new for this stage: convexity, conversion of the committed
  Blender export, the validation rules, `vehicle.json` parsing, collision bounds and ride height, and the drawn model.
- Physics measurements (13 probes plus the EV/SUV comparison) of the shipped SUV, the EV and the built-in vehicle
  are identical to v0.19.0.
- With the Blender body, the test SUV settles at the same ride height (0.8005 m) as the procedural SUV, and its
  braking, surface and climb measurements are identical. On the twister, its runs differ from 3.4 s on, where the
  two collision shapes differ.
- Scenario checks with 0 failures: full regression 247, Blender body 10, car views 3 and 4, wheel fit 13,
  suspension 2, EV 52.

## How to open it

**macOS.** The app is **ad-hoc signed, not notarized**, so Gatekeeper blocks a plain double-click after downloading.

1. Unzip `TrailForge-0.21.0-macos-arm64.zip`.
2. Either right-click `TrailForge.app` → **Open** → **Open**, or remove the quarantine flag in Terminal:
   `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine TrailForge.app`
3. Optional command-line options (run the binary inside the bundle):
   `TrailForge.app/Contents/MacOS/trailforge --help`

**Windows (untested on real hardware).** Unzip, keep the `assets` folder next to `trailforge.exe`, run it. The exe
is not code-signed, so SmartScreen may warn ("More info" → "Run anyway"). Needs a Direct3D 12 or Vulkan GPU driver.

**Linux (untested on real hardware).** `tar xzf TrailForge-0.21.0-linux-x64.tar.gz`, then
`./TrailForge-0.21.0-linux-x64/trailforge`. Needs a Vulkan driver (Mesa RADV / ANV / NVK or the NVIDIA driver).

## Known limitations

- The only Blender body is a blocky scripted test body, judged on still captures only. No shipped vehicle uses a
  Blender body yet.
- The game draws only the full-detail mesh of a Blender body. The simplified LODs are written and checked but not
  used yet. Textures are ignored: the contract is colour only.
- A Blender body does not refit to tire sizes changed outside its definition. The procedural bodies still do.
- Windows and Linux have never been run on real hardware; the Direct3D 12 path has not been run at all.
- macOS build not notarized; Windows build not code-signed.
- Earlier limitations still apply:
  - water has no physics; deep water and the map edge put the vehicle back on a road;
  - snow, sand and mud are rigid surfaces with lower grip;
  - grass, ground cover, spray and body mud are render-only;
  - ABS and TC are simplified logic;
  - sounds were judged from capture analysis, not by ear;
  - nobody has driven with a real gamepad;
  - TAA, motion blur, clouds and waves were judged on stills only;
  - buildings were judged on still captures only and have no interiors.
