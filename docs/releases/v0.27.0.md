TrailForge v0.27.0, an off-road vehicle prototype on a small custom C++20 engine (SDL3 + SDL GPU, Jolt Physics
`WheeledVehicleController`, Dear ImGui, own audio mixer on miniaudio). This stage adds a desert patrol buggy, two more
cars (IRONCLAD G and KESTREL M), three tire sets with a tire temperature model, asphalt on the Ironwall Stadium infield
islands, and a rounder Exploration Peaks map edge.

**Platforms:**

| Package | Status |
|---|---|
| `TrailForge-0.27.0-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS 27 on Apple Silicon. **Run and verified** (built for macOS 27.0). |

There are no Windows or Linux packages for this release: CI is paused (GitHub Actions billing), so nothing was
built or tested on those platforms. Windows and Linux have never been run on real hardware in any release.

**Private build:** Ironwall Stadium and the IRONCLAD G and KESTREL M bodies are third-party content. Their licence is
unknown or missing. They are included only while the repository is private, and they will be removed or replaced
before the repository or any build is made public. Do not share this package outside the project.

## What is new

- **Desert Patrol Buggy** (vehicle card **Desert Patrol Buggy**). A two-seat tube-frame buggy, built procedurally from
  the game's own body kit. It has an open sand-coloured cage, a light bar, a cargo bed with the spare lying flat, and
  no fenders, so the wheels, lower arms and orange coil-overs are exposed. The coil-overs are mounted on shock towers
  welded to the frame, and the arms and shocks follow the physics wheel every frame. 750 kg, on-demand AWD, about
  32 cm of suspension travel, a high-revving 112 N·m engine (limiter 11800 rpm) with six speeds, and no ABS or traction
  control by default (B / T turn them on). Measured with the headless probe: 0–100 km/h in 5.48 s, 100–0 km/h in
  46.43 m without ABS and 41.75 m with ABS.
- **Tire sets OFF-ROAD / ROAD / SLICK** (garage → Tires → tire set; changing it rebuilds the vehicle). OFF-ROAD is
  each vehicle's own tire and behaves exactly as before. ROAD and SLICK have more grip on asphalt and concrete and less
  on loose ground. Each tire has a two-node temperature model, one node for the tread surface and one for the carcass.
  The tires heat up from sliding, rolling and carcass deflection, and cool down through air, the ground and the rim.
  Grip depends on each set's temperature window (SLICK 80–110 °C, ROAD 40–95 °C), and the pressure follows the
  temperature. The coefficients were calibrated to published tire-thermal research (heat split, carcass heating,
  tread–carcass conduction, construction mass per set). A HUD widget and the telemetry show the temperature of each
  tire. The ABS and traction-control slip targets now scale with the set's peak slip.
  - Honest scale of the heating: in limit-driver probe laps a KESTREL M on slicks reaches about **61 °C** tread
    average after 3 laps, which is below the slick window. Slicks reach 80 °C or more only in sustained limit driving
    (150 s on a limit circle: 66–86 °C average depending on the car) or when they start warm off warmers. Cold slicks
    have less grip than the ROAD set.
  - 60–0 km/h with ABS on asphalt, warm tires: SUV OFF-ROAD 15.69 m, SLICK 13.30 m; KESTREL M ROAD 13.07 m, SLICK
    12.51 m.
- **IRONCLAD G** (boxy off-roader, 2560 kg, twin-turbo V8, permanent AWD, OFF-ROAD tires by default) and
  **KESTREL M** (rear-drive coupe, 1740 kg, twin-turbo six, ROAD tires by default). Their bodies are third-party
  models with an unknown licence (see "Private build" above). The physics come from published data, and the wheels and
  coil-overs come from the game's own physics as for every vehicle. Every badge, emblem and model lettering was
  replaced with blank discs or painted out, including the number plates. The arches leave no room for lowering, so
  the garage ride height only raises them.
- **Ironwall Stadium: the two infield islands are asphalt now.** They were classified as grass (low grip, soil tire
  marks). Inside the walls the drivable area is now only asphalt plus concrete structures, and a level test checks
  this every 1 m.
- **Exploration Peaks map edge.** From high up the world no longer ends in a square. The horizon ring is now round and
  reaches 24 km out, and it continues the level's coast with bays and small islands. The white blurry band in the
  north-west is gone: snow beyond the edge now follows the level's own snow line. The ring is render-only, and the
  play area is unchanged. Ironwall Stadium and Suncoast Raceway no longer show a pale sea border.

## How to open it

**macOS.** The app is **ad-hoc signed, not notarized**, so Gatekeeper blocks a plain double-click after downloading.

1. Unzip `TrailForge-0.27.0-macos-arm64.zip`.
2. Either right-click `TrailForge.app` → **Open** → **Open**, or remove the quarantine flag in Terminal:
   `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine TrailForge.app`
3. Optional command-line options (run the binary inside the bundle):
   `TrailForge.app/Contents/MacOS/trailforge --help`

## Known limitations

- IRONCLAD G, KESTREL M and Ironwall Stadium are third-party content with an unknown or missing licence (private
  build only, see above). The badges are removed, but the cars' body and grille designs are still recognisable.
- Nobody has driven the buggy, IRONCLAD G, KESTREL M or the tire sets by hand or with a gamepad. They were checked
  only with probes, autopilot laps, tests and still captures. Their engine sounds were not judged by ear.
- Tire temperatures are probably still 15–25 K below track-day readings, and they were not checked against measured
  data for these cars. The fitted coefficients come from racing and student-formula tires. The autopilot does not
  trail-brake or rotate the car like a human at the limit. There is no temperature across the tread, no wear, no wet
  ground and no brake heat, and the pressure is telemetry only.
- The SUV's warm-slick stop (about 1.1 g) is limited by its brake torque, not by the tires.
- KESTREL M takes 4.96 s from 0 to 100 km/h in the probe, slower than the real car's published figure (no launch
  control, limited by the rear tires).
- The buggy's arm and shock geometry is visual; camber change and motion ratio are not simulated. The buggy was not
  run in the open-world level.
- The Exploration Peaks horizon ring has no collision and low detail up close; distant islands look like flat
  plateaus from far above.
- The package grows by about 26 MB (the two vehicle bodies).
- No Windows or Linux packages for this release; those platforms have never been run on real hardware.
- macOS build not notarized.
- Earlier limitations still apply:
  - water has no physics; deep water and the map edge put the vehicle back on a road;
  - snow, sand, mud and gravel are rigid surfaces with lower grip;
  - grass, ground cover, spray and body mud are render-only;
  - ABS and TC are simplified logic;
  - sounds were judged from capture analysis, not by ear;
  - nobody has driven with a real gamepad;
  - buildings have no interiors.
