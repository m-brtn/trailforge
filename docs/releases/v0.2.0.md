Second packaged build of TrailForge, an off-road vehicle prototype on a small custom C++20 engine
(SDL3 + SDL GPU on Metal, Jolt Physics `WheeledVehicleController`, Dear ImGui, own audio mixer on miniaudio).
This release is about tire grip: launches no longer fake wheelspin, and loose surfaces are now slippery
enough to spin the wheels.

**Platform:** macOS 27 on Apple Silicon (arm64) only. The binary is built for macOS 27.0
(`LSMinimumSystemVersion` 27.0); nothing else has been run.

## Changes since v0.1.0

- **Tire squeal and launch wheelspin fixed.** Jolt caps the tire force at μ(slip of the previous step) × load, and
  the tire curve is 0 at zero slip, so a gripping wheel alternated between grip and free spin every physics step.
  Every launch looked and sounded like wheelspin. A tire that has not broken away can now pass up to its peak
  friction. Near idle the clutch also passes at most the engine torque at the current throttle (at least a 40 N·m
  creep torque); before, the slipping clutch delivered ~840 N·m at any throttle. Measured on concrete: full-throttle
  launch max slip 4.50 → 0.00 m/s, squeal 0.32 → 0.00; full-lock turn at 30 km/h squeal 0.37 → 0.00.
- **Squeal and rubber marks share one per-wheel gate.** Wheelspin or lock-up 1–5 m/s at slip ratio 0.2–0.55, or
  sideways sliding 2.5–7 m/s at 0.4–0.8. Only concrete, rock and the low-grip patch squeal; dirt, grass and mud
  have their own sounds. Soil picked up in the tread on loose ground suppresses both for ~10 m. Each wheel has its
  own squeal voice (random loop start and pitch per slide, pitch from sliding speed and load, an onset chirp).
- **Surface friction retuned.** The tire curve now peaks at μ 1.00, and the surface multiplier is the effective
  peak μ. Old values (tire 1.20 × multiplier) in brackets:

  | Surface | Peak μ longitudinal / lateral |
  |---|---|
  | Concrete | 0.95 / 0.95 (1.20 / 1.15) |
  | Rock | 0.75 / 0.75 (1.08 / 1.04) |
  | Packed dirt | 0.70 / 0.68 (1.02 / 0.98) |
  | Grass | 0.50 / 0.48 (0.96 / 0.90) |
  | Mud | 0.35 / 0.32 (0.72 / 0.63) |
  | Low-grip patch | 0.42 / 0.40 (unchanged) |

  Measured headless at 120 Hz, full throttle, before → after:
  - Concrete launches stay clean (max slip 0.27 m/s, 0–30 km/h 1.52 s).
  - Grass and mud now spin (max slip 7.3 / 9.4 m/s; 0–30 km/h 2.20 / 3.07 s, was 1.52 s).
  - Stopping from 50 km/h takes 13.7 m on concrete (was 12.0 m), 18.5 m on dirt and 36.2 m on mud. Without ABS all
    four wheels now lock under a full brake on concrete.
  - In low range, a full-throttle hill start on a 15° or 20° mud ramp spins in place. Every surface is still rigid:
    mud and grass only have lower friction.
- **Debug toggles** (F1 → Tuning): "Static grip" under Tires and "Launch clutch limit" (with the creep torque)
  under Drivetrain switch the two fixes on and off on the running vehicle, applied between physics steps.

## How to open it

The app is **ad-hoc signed, not notarized**, so Gatekeeper blocks a plain double-click after downloading.

1. Unzip `TrailForge-0.2.0-macos-arm64.zip`.
2. Either right-click `TrailForge.app` → **Open** → **Open**, or remove the quarantine flag in Terminal:
   `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine TrailForge.app`
3. Optional command-line options (run the binary inside the bundle):
   `TrailForge.app/Contents/MacOS/trailforge --help`

## Known limitations

- macOS 27 / Apple Silicon only; Metal only. Not notarized.
- The ground is rigid. Mud and grass are lower-friction surfaces, not soil: no sinkage, no rolling resistance, and
  spinning tires do not dig in. Puddles have no water physics; tire marks and vehicle dirt are visual only. No
  damage, no aerodynamics.
- The friction values are plausible ranges, not measured tire data. There is no traction control: once a tire
  spins it uses the sliding friction (μ × 0.80), so full throttle on a slippery slope spins where a careful throttle
  might climb. The tire grips without slip until it breaks away, instead of following a slip-stiffness curve.
- At a 60 Hz physics rate, a full-throttle concrete launch still slips the front wheels for one or two steps
  (120 Hz is the default).
- The 35° dirt hill now has almost no grip margin (μ 0.70 ≈ tan 35°), so the climb depends on the run-up.
- With auto reverse, holding S on a slope backs the vehicle down after it stops. Use the handbrake to hold it, or
  turn auto reverse off in F1 → Tuning → Simulation.
- Handling and sound have not been judged by hand or by ear; the squeal changes were judged from audio captures,
  and all checks are automated (scenario scripts and headless measurements). Gamepad support has not been tested
  with a real controller.
