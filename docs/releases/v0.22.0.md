TrailForge v0.22.0, an off-road vehicle prototype on a small custom C++20 engine (SDL3 + SDL GPU, Jolt Physics
`WheeledVehicleController`, Dear ImGui, own audio mixer on miniaudio). This stage is the third part of the **world
look** work on Exploration Peaks: steep ground without straight walls, layered rock, scree, a darker forest floor
and hills on the horizon. The vehicle physics did not change. The level ground changed where the walls were, and
collision is still the same height field as the visible ground.

## What is new

- **No more straight walls.** The long striped dirt walls came from hard height limits in the level bake, not from
  tile seams. These are now gone or softened:
  - the lake embankment is a sloped bank with uneven fall-off;
  - river banks wobble, and steep banks get an apron, terraces and rounded rims;
  - road cuts are eased to at most 1 m of height change per metre;
  - the coast no longer ends in a 64 m step at the edge of the map.

  A new test keeps dry ground above 80° below 0.04 % of the play area (now 0.000 %; before, 0.083 %).
- **Rock.** Steep faces use rock projected from three sides with a warped pattern, so it no longer stretches into
  stripes. The rock has dipping and folded layers, grey beds, large-scale colour variation and small ledges in the
  shading. Cliff models are placed more densely along steep faces, but not where water lies right below them.
- **Scree.** Clusters of boulders (the existing CC0 rock models, each with its own collision mesh) at the foot of
  walls at least 6 m high.
- **Forest floor.** The ground under the tree canopy is darker needle litter with extra shading.
- **Horizon.** The distant ring now has rolling hills and ridges, rising to a mountain range far out, in brighter
  colours.
- **Scenic views.** The lake shore view now shows the lake. The old view of the bank, a forest floor view and two
  horizon views were added.
- **Performance.** Trees switch to their simpler models closer to the camera than before. Frame rates at
  2880 × 1800 over five views (best of two runs on a shared machine; 120 fps is the display cap):

  | Preset | Range | Lowest view |
  |---|---|---|
  | Low | 120 fps and above | — |
  | Medium | 103–184 fps | forest |
  | High | 66–121 fps | forest, 66.2 fps (v0.21.0: 61.6) |
  | Ultra | 45–116 fps | forest |

## How to open it

**macOS.** The app is **ad-hoc signed, not notarized**, so Gatekeeper blocks a plain double-click after downloading.

1. Unzip `TrailForge-0.22.0-macos-arm64.zip`.
2. Either right-click `TrailForge.app` → **Open** → **Open**, or remove the quarantine flag in Terminal:
   `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine TrailForge.app`
3. Optional command-line options (run the binary inside the bundle):
   `TrailForge.app/Contents/MacOS/trailforge --help`

**Windows (untested on real hardware).** Unzip, keep the `assets` folder next to `trailforge.exe`, run it. The exe
is not code-signed, so SmartScreen may warn ("More info" → "Run anyway"). Needs a Direct3D 12 or Vulkan GPU driver.

**Linux (untested on real hardware).** `tar xzf TrailForge-0.22.0-linux-x64.tar.gz`, then
`./TrailForge-0.22.0-linux-x64/trailforge`. Needs a Vulkan driver (Mesa RADV / ANV / NVK or the NVIDIA driver).

## Known limitations

- Everything new was judged on still captures only, not in motion or while driving.
- From high above, the square edge of the baked level still shows against the horizon ring, which has no trees.
- At the Falls Pool rim, cliff slabs seen edge-on from above look like dark shards.
- Canyon terrace steps can still be up to about 7 m and nearly vertical (intended).
- The rock layers are shading on the height field, not geometry.
- Frame rates were measured on a machine shared with other work. Single cells differed by up to 40 fps between runs.
- Windows and Linux have never been run on real hardware; the Direct3D 12 path has not been run at all.
- macOS build not notarized; Windows build not code-signed.
- Earlier limitations still apply:
  - water has no physics; deep water and the map edge put the vehicle back on a road;
  - snow, sand and mud are rigid surfaces with lower grip;
  - grass, ground cover, spray and body mud are render-only;
  - ABS and TC are simplified logic;
  - sounds were judged from capture analysis, not by ear;
  - nobody has driven with a real gamepad;
  - TAA, motion blur, clouds and waves were judged on stills only;
  - buildings have no interiors; Blender bodies are a test path only.
