TrailForge v0.26.0, an off-road vehicle prototype on a small custom C++20 engine (SDL3 + SDL GPU, Jolt Physics
`WheeledVehicleController`, Dear ImGui, own audio mixer on miniaudio). This stage adds a banked oval stadium level,
IRONWALL STADIUM, drift scoring, real asphalt and gravel surfaces, tire marks drawn from the level's ground textures,
and a close follow camera.

**Platforms:**

| Package | Status |
|---|---|
| `TrailForge-0.26.0-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS 27 on Apple Silicon. **Run and verified** (built for macOS 27.0). |

There are no Windows or Linux packages for this release: CI is paused (GitHub Actions billing), so nothing was
built or tested on those platforms. Windows and Linux have never been run on real hardware in any release.

## What is new

- **Ironwall Stadium** (LEVEL row: **IRONWALL STADIUM**; fictional name). A short banked oval with concrete walls,
  tyre barriers, grandstands, an asphalt infield with a drift course, pits, a paddock and parking. The ground and
  structures are static meshes converted from a Blender scene, and the vehicle collides with exactly the triangles you
  see. Surfaces come from the materials: asphalt, grass, gravel, and concrete for the structures. Cones, clipping-point
  boards, painted lines and tree cards are visual only. The lap timer runs on a 424 m racing line, counter-clockwise,
  banked 5–14°, with 6 gates and 3 sectors. The map lists three POIs: Start / Finish, Infield, Paddock.
- **Drift scoring.** Scored on body slip angle × speed × time, with a multiplier inside clipping zones. A run ends
  after 1.5 s without drifting. It fails (0 points) on a spin or a wall hit, and is dropped on a reset. The HUD panel
  shows the slip angle, speed, running score, zone, the last result and the best score. Best completed runs are
  stored per level and vehicle. On Ironwall Stadium the course is the oval plus the infield, with ×2 zones along the
  outer walls (2.5 m from the wall) and ×1.5 zones at 12 inner clipping points. The proving ground mini track has its
  own zones. Switch: F1 → Simulation → Drift scoring (World setting / On / Off).
- **Asphalt and gravel surfaces.** Asphalt (μ 1.00) and loose gravel (lower, flatter grip curve, extra rolling
  resistance; still rigid ground) are new physics surfaces. They are used on the mini handling track, Suncoast
  Raceway (asphalt and gravel traps), the stadium and three gravel roads of Exploration Peaks, and they drive tire
  marks, spray, audio, the UI and the map.
- **Tire marks on levels** follow the photo-textured ground under them instead of painting flat bands. On gravel they
  show compressed stones with tread and a ragged edge. Proving ground marks are unchanged.
- **Close follow camera** between follow and hood in the C / RB cycle (follow → close follow → hood → free). It is
  sized from the vehicle bounds, sits nearer and higher than the follow view, looks 12° down with the road ahead in
  view, and tilts with the body's smoothed pitch.
- The map shows the size of levels under 1 km in metres.

## How to open it

**macOS.** The app is **ad-hoc signed, not notarized**, so Gatekeeper blocks a plain double-click after downloading.

1. Unzip `TrailForge-0.26.0-macos-arm64.zip`.
2. Either right-click `TrailForge.app` → **Open** → **Open**, or remove the quarantine flag in Terminal:
   `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine TrailForge.app`
3. Optional command-line options (run the binary inside the bundle):
   `TrailForge.app/Contents/MacOS/trailforge --help`

## Known limitations

- Ironwall Stadium is third-party content without a licence (private build only, see above). Its sponsor artwork
  belongs to its owners.
- Nobody has driven the stadium or scored a drift by hand; only the autopilot laps and one scripted slide. The drift
  zones are placed from the scene geometry, not tuned by driving. The stadium meshes have no normal maps, and the walls
  and tyre stacks are rigid.
- Gravel is a rigid surface with a loose-surface grip curve: no sinkage and no displaced stones.
- The close follow camera was not judged in motion by hand or with a real gamepad.
- The package grows by about 54 MB (stadium models and textures).
- No Windows or Linux packages for this release; those platforms have never been run on real hardware.
- macOS build not notarized.
- Earlier limitations still apply:
  - water has no physics; deep water and the map edge put the vehicle back on a road;
  - snow, sand and mud are rigid surfaces with lower grip;
  - grass, ground cover, spray and body mud are render-only;
  - ABS and TC are simplified logic;
  - sounds were judged from capture analysis, not by ear;
  - nobody has driven with a real gamepad;
  - buildings have no interiors; Blender bodies are a test path only.
