Third packaged build of TrailForge, an off-road vehicle prototype on a small custom C++20 engine
(SDL3 + SDL GPU on Metal, Jolt Physics `WheeledVehicleController`, Dear ImGui, own audio mixer on miniaudio).
This release is about what the wheels leave behind: soil carried in the tread, prints on hard ground, a new sand
area, a fixed skid-mark profile, and cosmetic wheel spray.

**Platform:** macOS 27 on Apple Silicon (arm64) only. The binary is built for macOS 27.0
(`LSMinimumSystemVersion` 27.0); nothing else has been run.

## Changes since v0.2.0

- **Mud → asphalt "fat mark" fixed.** Leaving mud (or any soft ground) for a hard surface with wheelspin or
  lock-up left a short, wide, black rubber blob that ended abruptly. Cause: the segment that crossed the boundary
  took the style of the new surface (rubber) but kept the old values — full mud churn (slip 1.00) and the 1.3× mud
  width — while the next segment was nearly invisible. Now a ribbon never spans two surfaces: it ends in the old
  surface's style and a new one starts. A headless test drives across the boundary rolling, spinning and locked.
- **Tread soil.** Each wheel carries soil (amount, colour, wetness): mud loads the tread fully and wet, packed dirt
  half and dry, sand light and dusty, grass greenish. On hard ground the tread prints it as a fading lug pattern
  and sheds it. Measured on synthetic samples: after wet mud the print starts at 0.94 and is visible for 18.5 m
  rolling (4–6 m with wheelspin or lock-up); after dirt 0.22 for 6.5 m, after sand 0.15 for 4.9 m. The wheels show
  the soil too (tread and grooves first, then the sidewall, the rim barely).
- **Sand area** in the east meadow (x 28–45, z 48–84): a rigid `Sand` surface (peak μ 0.55 / 0.52), flat next to the
  skid strip, with low dunes further north. It leaves soft sand marks; its tire sound uses the dirt layer.
- **Mud bath and west strip:** a flat 6 × 12 m mud patch that ends directly on a 30 m concrete strip, for driving
  out of mud onto concrete. Teleports: "Sand area (dunes)", "Sand to concrete", "Mud bath to concrete",
  "Meadow (grass)", "Puddle (splash)".
- **Skid-mark profile fixed.** A locked-wheel stop now starts faint, stays even, and does not darken toward the
  end. The rubber builds up with patch heat over ~0.5 s and scales with √load. In a 100 → 0 km/h stop at
  60/120/240 Hz the cover is 0.08–0.10 at the start, 0.99 in the middle (min 0.91–0.93) and 1.00 over the last 2 m,
  with no in-place stamps. Before, the lightly loaded rears locked first and the loaded fronts laid a darker ribbon
  over them about 13 m later.
- **Wheel spray (cosmetic).** The wheels throw clods, crumbs, sand grains, dust, grass bits and puddle drops,
  driven by slip per surface. Spinning wheels throw a fan backward and up, locked wheels push material ahead, and
  wheels rolling calmly throw almost nothing. Concrete, rock and low grip throw nothing. These are simple CPU
  particles, **not physics bodies**: gravity, drag and a collision with the ground height only. They never affect
  the vehicle. One instanced draw lit by the scene; the cap depends on the quality preset (Low 600 without dust,
  Medium 1500, High 3000, Ultra 6000). With every particle on screen at the cap, the GPU frame time measured
  3.8 / 6.7 / 6.5 / 8.2 ms on Low / Medium / High / Ultra (2880×1800 output).

## Controls

| Action | Keyboard | Gamepad |
|---|---|---|
| Throttle / brake (hold S at a stop: reverse) | W / S | RT / LT |
| Steering | A / D | left stick |
| Handbrake (holds the vehicle on a slope) | Space | A |
| Forward ↔ reverse by hand (when stopped) | Q | X |
| Low range (when stopped) | L | Y |
| Reset to the start point | R | Back |
| Follow / free camera | C | RB |
| Headlights | H | — |
| Diagnostics / graphics / audio panels | F1 / F2 / F3 | — |
| Pause / single physics step | Esc / N | Start |
| Orbit / zoom camera | right mouse + drag / wheel | — |

Teleports to every test feature are in F1 → Teleport. Wheel spray and tire marks have their own controls in
F2 → Graphics.

## How to open it

The app is **ad-hoc signed, not notarized**, so Gatekeeper blocks a plain double-click after downloading.

1. Unzip `TrailForge-0.3.0-macos-arm64.zip`.
2. Either right-click `TrailForge.app` → **Open** → **Open**, or remove the quarantine flag in Terminal:
   `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine TrailForge.app`
3. Optional command-line options (run the binary inside the bundle):
   `TrailForge.app/Contents/MacOS/trailforge --help`

## Known limitations

- macOS 27 / Apple Silicon only; Metal only. Not notarized.
- The ground is still rigid. Sand and mud are lower-friction surfaces: no sinkage, no rolling resistance, and
  spinning tires do not dig in. Puddles have no water physics. Tire marks, tread soil, prints, vehicle dirt and
  wheel spray are visual only: they never change grip or the ground.
- Spray particles do not collide with the vehicle, rocks or each other and take no material from the ground. In a
  puddle the water drops are hard to tell apart from the mud clods. The spray was judged only from still captures,
  never watched in motion by a person.
- The sand area has a straight edge to the meadow (no blend).
- The soil print lengths were measured on synthetic contact samples; in the app they were only looked at in
  captures.
- The 0.45 m step is a borderline climb in low range: in one of the automated `full_check` runs during this
  stage the vehicle stalled against it (the rerun climbed it).
- Everything else from v0.2.0 still applies: the friction values are plausible ranges, not measured tire data,
  and there is no traction control. At 60 Hz a concrete launch slips the front wheels for one or two steps. With
  auto reverse, holding S on a slope backs the vehicle down; use the handbrake.
- Handling and sound have not been judged by hand or by ear. All checks are automated (scenario scripts and
  headless measurements). Gamepad support has not been tested with a real controller.
