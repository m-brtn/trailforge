TrailForge v0.16.0, an off-road vehicle prototype on a small custom C++20 engine (SDL3 + SDL GPU, Jolt Physics
`WheeledVehicleController`, Dear ImGui, own audio mixer on miniaudio). This stage adds a **map with a waypoint, a
compass bar and a minimap**, and the vehicle now **drives a loop behind the start menu**.

**Platforms:**

| Package | Status |
|---|---|
| `TrailForge-0.16.0-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS 27 on Apple Silicon. **Run and verified** (built for macOS 27.0). |
| `TrailForge-0.16.0-windows-x64.zip` | **UNTESTED ON REAL HARDWARE.** Built and headless-tested in GitHub Actions (MSVC, x64); the D3D12 / Vulkan rendering has never been run. |
| `TrailForge-0.16.0-linux-x64.tar.gz` | **UNTESTED ON REAL HARDWARE.** Built and headless-tested in GitHub Actions (gcc, Ubuntu); the app was only started on Mesa lavapipe (software Vulkan) under Xvfb, without loading the level. |

## What is new

- **Full-screen map.** Open it with **M**, gamepad **LB**, or **MAP** in the pause menu; the game pauses while it is
  open. It is drawn from the level data: hill shading, 10 m contours, lakes, rivers and the sea by depth, forests,
  roads and trails, bridges, and the 13 points of interest as numbered markers with names. A side list shows each
  POI with its distance. It also has a scale bar, a north arrow and your vehicle. The proving ground has its own map
  of the track, with its 16 features.
- **Waypoint.** Click the map, or press A at the crosshair, to set a waypoint (it snaps to a nearby POI); click it
  again to clear it. Zoom with the wheel, Q / E or the triggers. Pan by dragging, with W A S D / the arrows, or with
  the left stick. C or Y centers the map on the vehicle. **There is no fast travel**: the map only sets the waypoint.
- **Compass bar** at the top of the screen. It shows your heading, the nearby POIs, and the waypoint with its
  distance; when the waypoint is behind you, it is pinned to the edge of the bar with an arrow.
- **Minimap** in the bottom-right corner. It turns with the vehicle (or stays north up) and shows a guidance line to
  the waypoint. Settings → MAP turns the minimap and the compass on or off, sets the minimap size (small / medium /
  large) and its orientation; the choices are saved.
- **Attract drive.** Behind the intro and the start menu the vehicle drives a loop through the valley by itself.
  It uses only throttle, brake and steering, through the same pedal smoothing as the player's: no added forces, no
  setting of the speed. A slow cinematic camera follows it. The vehicle page and the garage hold the vehicle still.
  Start puts it back on the spawn.
- **Measured** (Release, Apple Silicon):
  - The map is built on a background thread when a world loads: 15–17 ms for Exploration Peaks (2048², 1.09 m per
    pixel) and 9–11 ms for the proving ground.
  - The minimap and the compass bar showed no measurable GPU cost: 15.2 ms on vs 15.1 ms off at 2880 × 1800 High,
    within the ±0.5 ms run-to-run noise.
  - The attract drive completes the 1550 m valley loop with the gasoline SUV in 2H and in 4H, and with the EV, never
    more than 0.75 m off the line.

## How to open it

**macOS.** The app is **ad-hoc signed, not notarized**, so Gatekeeper blocks a plain double-click after downloading.

1. Unzip `TrailForge-0.16.0-macos-arm64.zip`.
2. Either right-click `TrailForge.app` → **Open** → **Open**, or remove the quarantine flag in Terminal:
   `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine TrailForge.app`
3. Optional command-line options (run the binary inside the bundle):
   `TrailForge.app/Contents/MacOS/trailforge --help`

**Windows (untested on real hardware).** Unzip, keep the `assets` folder next to `trailforge.exe`, run it. The exe
is not code-signed, so SmartScreen may warn ("More info" → "Run anyway"). Needs a Direct3D 12 or Vulkan GPU driver.

**Linux (untested on real hardware).** `tar xzf TrailForge-0.16.0-linux-x64.tar.gz`, then
`./TrailForge-0.16.0-linux-x64/trailforge`. Needs a Vulkan driver (Mesa RADV / ANV / NVK or the NVIDIA driver).

## Known limitations

- The gamepad map controls (LB, triggers, stick, A, B, Y) were tested with a virtual SDL gamepad only; nobody has
  used them with a real pad.
- The map ground image is about 1 m per pixel, so it gets soft at the closest zoom (roads, labels and markers stay
  sharp). Rocks and tree crowns are drawn as simple dots.
- The waypoint is not saved between sessions or across a level change.
- On the map, a POI label that would cover another one is left out (its number and the list still show it).
- The attract drive is silent: the gameplay sounds stay muted behind the start menu (only the music plays). When it
  gets stuck, leaves the route or tips over, the vehicle is quietly put back at the route start (the only reset
  besides R, the F1 teleports and deep water / map edge recovery).
- Windows and Linux have never been run on real hardware; the Direct3D 12 path has not been run at all.
- macOS build not notarized; Windows build not code-signed.
- Earlier limitations still apply:
  - water has no physics; deep water and the map edge put the vehicle back on a road;
  - snow, sand and mud are rigid surfaces with lower grip;
  - grass, spray and body mud are render-only;
  - ABS and TC are simplified logic;
  - the car paint is blotchy up close;
  - sounds were judged from capture analysis, not by ear;
  - nobody has driven with a real gamepad.
