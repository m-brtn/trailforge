TrailForge v0.14.0, an off-road vehicle prototype on a small custom C++20 engine (SDL3 + SDL GPU, Jolt Physics
`WheeledVehicleController`, Dear ImGui, own audio mixer on miniaudio). This stage makes the **bridges** of
Exploration Peaks drivable on the wheels, adds a **hood camera** and a **right-stick camera**, moves **BACK** to the
top of every menu page, and draws **grass and trees much farther**.

**Platforms:**

| Package | Status |
|---|---|
| `TrailForge-0.14.0-macos-arm64.zip` | macOS 27 on Apple Silicon. **Run and verified** (built for macOS 27.0). |
| `TrailForge-0.14.0-windows-x64.zip` | **UNTESTED ON REAL HARDWARE.** Built and headless-tested in GitHub Actions (MSVC, x64); the D3D12 / Vulkan rendering has never been run. |
| `TrailForge-0.14.0-linux-x64.tar.gz` | **UNTESTED ON REAL HARDWARE.** Built and headless-tested in GitHub Actions (gcc, Ubuntu); the app was only started on Mesa lavapipe (software Vulkan) under Xvfb, without loading the level. |

## What is new

- **Bridges.** The wheels fell through the bridge decks while the body still hit them: the deck mesh was built
  inside-out, and Jolt's wheel tests ignore back faces. The decks, ramps and piers are now built the right way
  round. Both vehicles cross both bridges in both directions with all four wheels on the deck and no body contact
  (checked in a headless test and in the level scenario).
- **No grass on bridges and roads.** Grass, flowers and wheat no longer grow through the bridge ramps or creep onto
  the roads. Trees, rocks, houses and props also keep clear of roads and bridges.
- **Hood camera.** C / RB now cycles follow → hood → free. The hood camera sits on the roof line in front of the
  windshield and moves with the body. The hood fills the bottom 18% of the view (both vehicles, flat ground and a
  slope). The mount is set per vehicle in `vehicle.json` (`camera.hood`).
- **Right stick.** In the follow view it orbits the camera, which swings back behind the vehicle 1.75 s after you
  let go while driving. In the hood view it looks around and springs back. With several pads connected, the
  newest one is used.
- **Menus.** Every page has a **◀ BACK** button at the top left, above the title. You can click it, or press Up
  from the first row to reach it. It is never selected by default, so Enter does not go back by accident. Esc / B
  still go back. The Controls page no longer lists the menu keys and lists the right stick.
- **Draw distances.** New settings in Settings → Graphics and in the F2 panel, saved in `settings.cfg`:
  - **Grass distance:** PRESET, or 30–250 m.
  - **Tree detail distance:** PRESET, or 250–2000 m.

  New preset values:

  | Preset | Grass | Tree detail | Trees drawn to |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Low | 40 m (was 22) | 380 m | 2 km (was 1.5) |
  | Medium | 70 m (was 32) | 500 m (was 380) | 3 km (was 2.5) |
  | High | 110 m (was 45) | 650 m (was 380) | 3.5 km |
  | Ultra | 160 m (was 60) | 900 m (was 380) | 4.5 km |

  Grass shrinks away smoothly at its limit. Trees blend between their detail levels with a fine dither instead of
  switching at once.
- **Cost of the new distances** (Exploration Peaks, 2880 × 1800, Metal, vsync off, GPU time per frame, new vs old
  distances in the same build):

  | View | High | Ultra |
  |---|---|---|
  | Forest trail | 6.9 vs 6.9 ms | 11.3 vs 9.2 ms |
  | Village | 7.6–11.0 vs 6.9–10.5 ms (noisy) | 13.7–17.5 vs 10.7–23 ms (noisy) |

  High stays well inside a 60 Hz frame (16.7 ms).
- **Scenario checks.** Scenarios now advance a fixed 1/120 s per frame, so they give the same results on 60 Hz and
  120 Hz displays, with vsync on or off. A start-up race that ignored a script's first teleport is fixed. It was
  also present in v0.13.0.

## How to open it

**macOS.** The app is **ad-hoc signed, not notarized**, so Gatekeeper blocks a plain double-click after downloading.

1. Unzip `TrailForge-0.14.0-macos-arm64.zip`.
2. Either right-click `TrailForge.app` → **Open** → **Open**, or remove the quarantine flag in Terminal:
   `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine TrailForge.app`
3. Optional command-line options (run the binary inside the bundle):
   `TrailForge.app/Contents/MacOS/trailforge --help`

**Windows (untested on real hardware).** Unzip, keep the `assets` folder next to `trailforge.exe`, run it. The exe
is not code-signed, so SmartScreen may warn ("More info" → "Run anyway"). Needs a Direct3D 12 or Vulkan GPU driver.

**Linux (untested on real hardware).** `tar xzf TrailForge-0.14.0-linux-x64.tar.gz`, then
`./TrailForge-0.14.0-linux-x64/trailforge`. Needs a Vulkan driver (Mesa RADV / ANV / NVK or the NVIDIA driver).

## Known limitations

- Windows and Linux have never been run on real hardware; the Direct3D 12 path has not been run at all.
- Nobody has driven with a gamepad: the right-stick camera was checked with a virtual SDL pad. A real pad was
  connected to the test Mac only while the pad selection was being fixed.
- The hood camera is not a cockpit view: there is no interior.
- Grass fading and the tree blending were checked on still captures, not watched in motion by a person.
- Rocks and props have a single level of detail, so there is no distance setting for them.
- The frame-time numbers were measured with other programs running; the village view varied a lot between runs.
- macOS build not notarized; Windows build not code-signed.
- Earlier limitations still apply:
  - water has no physics; deep water and the map edge put the vehicle back on a road;
  - snow, sand and mud are rigid surfaces with lower grip;
  - grass, spray and body mud are render-only;
  - ABS and TC are simplified logic;
  - sounds were judged from capture analysis, not by ear.
